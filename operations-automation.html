Operations & Automation

Reliable operations first. Automate where it genuinely improves the service.

My approach to IT operations combines clear ownership, supportability, resilience and practical automation. Automation should remove repetitive work, reduce risk and improve consistency without creating a new layer of complexity that nobody owns.

See the operating model Explore practical work

Operating Philosophy

Automation supports the operating model; it does not replace it.

Strong operations start with knowing who owns a service, how it is supported, what happens when it fails and how change is controlled. Once those basics are understood, automation can make the service faster, more consistent and easier to operate.

1

Understand

Clarify the service, owner, process, dependencies, risk and support model.

2

Standardise

Reduce unnecessary variation and make the process clear enough to repeat.

3

Automate

Remove repetitive work where automation improves consistency, speed or control.

4

Monitor

Log outcomes, detect failure and keep operational ownership visible.

5

Improve

Use incidents, exceptions and feedback to refine the service over time.

Automation Principles

Good automation should make the environment easier to operate.

Keep ownership visible

A script or workflow should still have a clear owner, support path and recovery process.

Make failure observable

Logging, alerts and clear outcomes matter as much as the automation itself.

Prefer simple over clever

Automation that only one person understands can create a new operational dependency.

Principle

Automate the repeatable. Keep ownership visible.

The objective is not to maximise scripts or workflows. It is to reduce manual effort and risk while preserving clear accountability, logging, documentation and recovery paths.
