From Traditional Project Management to Agile and MVP

Evolving delivery without losing governance, testing, documentation or operational ownership

A practical reflection on moving from traditional stage-gated delivery toward Agile and MVP thinking without throwing away the controls that make enterprise change sustainable.

Traditional project management still has value

  • Business cases, decision gates, plans, RAID logs, budgets and clear accountability remain useful where risk, dependency and organisational coordination are high.
  • The problem is not structure; it is when structure becomes a substitute for feedback and learning.

What Agile changed

  • Shorter feedback loops make it easier to test assumptions before a full solution is locked in.
  • Backlogs, iterative delivery and regular demonstrations improve transparency when they are tied to business outcomes rather than activity.

What MVP changed

  • MVP thinking asks what is the smallest usable outcome that proves value or enables the next decision.
  • It helps challenge 'everything is critical' requirements and reduces the risk of spending months perfecting low-value scope.

What should not disappear

  • Security, architecture, data quality, testing, change management, documentation, cutover planning, support readiness and BAU ownership do not become optional because a team is Agile.
  • The delivery model should be proportionate to the risk and complexity of the change.

A pragmatic hybrid

  • Use enough governance to make decisions and risks visible.
  • Use iteration to learn early.
  • Use MVP to focus investment.
  • Use formal validation and operational transition where failure would materially affect customers, staff or the organisation.

Key lessons

  • Methodology is a tool, not an identity.
  • Agile should increase transparency, not remove accountability.
  • MVP means smallest valuable outcome, not lowest quality.
  • Enterprise delivery still needs a clear path from business case to BAU.
